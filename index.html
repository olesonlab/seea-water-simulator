What the Technical Committee is being asked to decide

The note proposes a method for valuing water resources under the SEEA Central Framework. It is written for the committee and reviewers deciding whether this becomes part of the international standard, and for statistical offices weighing what adopting it would cost them.

A handful of countries already compile some form of water account. Their methods differ enough that stock and flow estimates are not comparable across borders. No operational guidance currently resolves that.

Water accounts have uses well beyond national accounts integration. A river-basin-level monetary account tells a water ministry where scarcity is concentrated and what a drought would cost. It gives a finance ministry a basis for pricing reform and for weighing infrastructure against other capital spending. Valuing water depletion the way minerals and timber already are is one application of this work, not the reason for it. SNA 2025 para. 35.50 delegates natural resource depletion valuation to the SEEA CF update process, and water is the category that delegation left most unresolved.

Three principles carry through every recommendation. Water is valued as a resource, not a service: the aquifer or river is what gets valued, not a utility's revenue. Valuation is scarcity-driven, so the same cubic metre is worth more in a stressed basin than an abundant one. And scope follows use, not physical form, which is what the scope test in section 03 encodes.

Where reviewer input shapes the outcome most
  • Rec 1 · TC 5A scope test for what counts as a water resource. The committee is asked directly whether that test holds up against the SNA economic-asset criteria in practice, or needs further guidance.
  • Rec 4, 5A tiered valuation method. Market prices where observed, cost or productivity estimation where they are not, so a country with limited data has a defensible place to start rather than an all-or-nothing standard.
  • Rec 6River-basin compilation rather than national averages. A single national figure hides exactly the scarcity variation the account exists to capture.
  • Rec 7 · TC 4Water quality as a separate, documented adjustment to value, not folded silently into volume. The committee is asked whether this should be a distinct degradation cost at all.

Revaluation is a holding gain, not income. It records the change in value of the stock held at the start of the period, priced at the change in p̄. The opening stock carries the price change, not the closing stock: flows recorded during the period are priced separately at that period's own rate and would be double counted otherwise. Computing REV on the closing stock introduces a residual of ΔS × Δp̄ that breaks the account's own reconciliation, which is what Annex E Example 5 Scenario B demonstrates.

OCV is a balancing item with a diagnostic reading. A negative OCV, where Ft falls below qt × p̄, is the signal for administered pricing suppressing rent below scarcity value, and grounds for a Tier 2 method. That reading assumes V came from the full NPV chain. Under the Annex A constant-rent perpetuity shortcut, OCV is persistently nonzero by construction and should not on its own be read as a pricing signal.
