What the three recommendations say
Rec 2 — Valuation Targets: distinguish three separate valuation targets for water — stock value V (balance sheet), flow value Ft (MSUT), and depletion value Dt (NDP/NNI) — and specify which one each accounting purpose requires.
Rec 3 — MSUT and Asset Account Reconciliation: compile the monetary supply-use table and the water asset account jointly. MSUT values extraction at the resource rent Ft = Rt × qt; the asset account values it at qt × p̄. Report the gap between them as OCV, with a narrative explanation.
Rec 8 — Valuation at the Extremes: as a resource nears exhaustion, cap the depletion charge at the unit cost of the cheapest accessible substitute technology — no rational user would keep paying more than that to extract.

Annex E worked examples — reproduced by the Load-Example buttons (Ex 4 is reference-only, see below)
Ex 1 — Renewable aquifer, qt=10 > gt=8 km³/yr: Dt=Int$500M. The constant-rent perpetuity decouples p̄ from Rt, so OCVt=+Int$2,500M even under over-extraction.
Ex 2 — Fossil aquifer, gt=0: all extraction depletes. Dt=Int$5,000M exceeds Ft=Int$4,000M — wealth falls faster than income is earned.
Ex 3 — Over-extracted surface water: a smaller over-extraction volume than Ex 2, but a higher p̄=Int$2.25/m³, gives both a larger Dt (Int$6,750M) and a larger OCVt gap.
Ex 4 — Near exhaustion (S=16 km³, ΔS/S=31%): uncapped Dt would be Int$31,250M. Rec 8's substitute ceiling (psubstitute=Int$2.00/m³) caps it at Int$10,000M. This example tracks a stock trajectory at fixed V rather than a single qt/gt period, so it isn't one of the Load-Example presets — but the substitute-ceiling slider above models the same cap mechanism; push qt−gt and Rt up until p̄ exceeds psubstitute to see it bind.
Ex 5 — OCVt decomposition: falls from +Int$2,500M (Scenario A, Rt=0.50) to +Int$1,000M (Scenario B, Rt=0.20) as Rt drops, tracking the gap between current rent and the capitalized shadow price.
